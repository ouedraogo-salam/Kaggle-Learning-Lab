## 📝 Documentation Standards

Every resource added to this repository should be documented using a consistent structure.

### Information to record

- **Title:** Name of the notebook, article, paper, or resource.
- **Category:** Main Data Science topic.
- **Source:** Original URL.
- **Author:** Original author or creator, when available.
- **Date added:** Date the resource was added.
- **Resource type:** Notebook, article, research paper, tutorial, or code.
- **Difficulty:** Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced.
- **Key concepts:** Main techniques and ideas covered.
- **Summary:** A brief explanation of the resource.
- **Practical value:** What I can learn or apply from it.
- **Personal notes:** My observations, questions, and lessons learned.
- **Status:** To review, In progress, Understood, or Applied.

## 🔍 Resource Evaluation

Before adding a resource, I will consider the following questions:

1. What problem does this resource address?
2. Which concepts or techniques does it introduce?
3. Why is it useful or different from other resources?
4. Can I reproduce or adapt the proposed approach?
5. How could I apply it to a personal project or competition?
6. What are its limitations?

## 💡 Knowledge Acquisition Workflow

My approach to learning from a resource follows these steps:

1. **Discover:** Find an interesting resource on Kaggle or another reliable source.
2. **Evaluate:** Identify its relevance, quality, and potential applications.
3. **Document:** Record the source, summary, key concepts, and author.
4. **Understand:** Study the methodology and reproduce important examples when appropriate.
5. **Experiment:** Test the technique on a dataset or personal project.
6. **Reflect:** Document what worked, what did not, and what I learned.
7. **Reuse:** Link the knowledge to related resources and future projects.

## 📌 Important Principles

- Always preserve the original source and author attribution.
- Distinguish original content from personal notes and experiments.
- Do not copy or redistribute third-party notebooks or datasets without checking their licenses and usage permissions.
- Prefer linking to original resources when redistribution is not permitted.
- Document modifications when adapting existing code.
- Prioritize understanding and practical application over collecting resources without reviewing them.
- Keep the repository organized, searchable, and easy to maintain.
